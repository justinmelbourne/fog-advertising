Part 2 • Why We Need --fog-gray-50 (The Contrast Law)

You asked: "Can we merge --fog-gray-50 into --fog-gray, or is it best practice in modern web design to have tints?"

Here is the actual rendered visual and WCAG mathematical proof. If we use --fog-gray (#DCDDDE) as a card or panel background, Fog Blue text fails accessibility (3.95:1 < 4.5:1) and the card looks like a heavy concrete slab.

FAILS WCAG AA (3.95:1)

Fog Blue Headline on --fog-gray (#DCDDDE)

This background is #DCDDDE. Fog Blue text on this background has a contrast ratio of 3.95:1. WCAG AA requires at least 4.5:1 for normal text. It is illegal for public accessibility compliance.

Blue link fails contrast on this ground →
Visual result: Heavy, muddy "concrete slab" look. Doesn't feel like modern web design.
PASSES WCAG AA (4.80:1)

Fog Blue Headline on --fog-gray-50 (#F1F2F3)

This background is #F1F2F3. Fog Blue text on this background has a contrast ratio of 4.80:1, cleanly passing WCAG AA.

Blue link cleanly readable →
Visual result: Crisp, airy editorial card ground. Industry standard (Stripe, Apple, Tailwind).
Industry Best Practice Summary: All modern design systems (Tailwind, Stripe, Apple HIG, GitHub Primer) use a "Level 50" tint (95–98% luminance) for panel surfaces and cards. --fog-gray (#DCDDDE) is reserved for borders, hairlines, and badges. --fog-gray-50 (#F1F2F3) is the card & panel ground.
